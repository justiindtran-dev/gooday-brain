# Weight loss and GLP-1 trends, Nov 2026 (for the konjac jelly idea)

## The drugs
- About 500,000 Australians use GLP-1s regularly (sales-based minimum, UNSW, Jul 2026). Nearly half pay $200–$700/month privately. EY's survey puts self-reported use at 1 in 10 adults and projects 17–30% within a decade.
- Mounjaro is not on the PBS for anything. PBAC recommended it for type 2 diabetes in Mar 2026, but Lilly walked away over price in Apr 2026.
- Wegovy for people with heart disease and BMI 35+ went to PBAC on 4–6 Nov 2026. The outcome is due around 18 Dec 2026. Broad obesity subsidy would need a program outside the PBS.
- Compounded GLP-1s were banned from 1 Oct 2024. Telehealth is now the main private channel.
- Pills are here in the US: Wegovy pill (Jan 2026) and Lilly's Foundayo/orforglipron (approved 1 Apr 2026), both from US$149/month cash. 57% of Australians surveyed would prefer a pill. Expect cheaper, wider use when they reach Australia.

## How users eat and shop
- Users eat less but don't spend less on food. Spending shifts to protein (+11%), fruit and veg (+15%), protein/fibre supplements (~+20%). Candy (-14%), snacks, soft drinks and alcohol drop about 10% (BCG, Aug 2026).
- Australian users dine out 44% less and drink 41% less alcohol (EY, Jul 2026).
- 7 in 10 users are trying to improve gut health, most by adding fibre. Users want small "portion-right" formats and "controlled indulgence" (Givaudan/FIS, Nov 2026).
- "GLP-1 friendly" badges are met with scepticism. Users want credible, plain claims.
- It spreads through households: half or more of users say their family's eating changed too.
- About a third of past users have stopped at any time, and many cycle on and off. They worry about regaining weight.

## Fads
- "Nature's Ozempic" claims (berberine etc.) are everywhere but are hype. They draw clinical and regulatory pushback.

## What it means for the jelly
- Fit: low-cal, fibre (glucomannan), small treat portion, sweet craving without sugar. That matches "controlled indulgence" and fibre/gut health.
- Positioning to test: (1) companion for users who get small appetites and constipation, (2) "off-ramp" for people stopping the drug, (3) the bigger non-drug group copying the habits.
- Add protein if you can. Protein plus fibre is the strongest current pitch.
- Avoid "natural Ozempic" or GLP-1 mimic claims. They are weak scientifically and risky under food health-claim and ACCC rules.

## Big regulatory warning
- The ACCC permanently bans mini jelly cups containing konjac (glucomannan) 45 mm or less in height or width (Consumer Protection Notice No. 2 of 2004). Larger spoon-eaten cups are allowed. Pouch/drinkable formats are not addressed on the ban page, so get advice before choosing a format.
