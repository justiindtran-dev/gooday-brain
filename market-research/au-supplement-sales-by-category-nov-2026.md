# Australian supplement sales by category (as of Nov 2026)

**Main source:** Complementary Medicines Australia (CMA) Industry Snapshot 2026 (Nov 2026), which uses Euromonitor International Consumer Health data. Values are AUD retail value. "2026" values are Euromonitor's estimates for the full year. Some figures from the back half of that PDF were read from a summary, so check the original before quoting them to investors.

Labels used below:
- **Hard**: retail value or survey data from Euromonitor/ABS, reported by CMA.
- **Estimate**: a paid research-firm model (IMARC, FMI, etc.). These often disagree with each other by 2x to 10x because each firm draws the scope differently. Use them for direction only.

Across the whole market (supplements + sports + weight), online is 27.9% of 2026 sales, up from 12.8% in 2019, and delivered 54% of all growth since 2019. **Sports nutrition is 40.1% online.**

## 6. Consumer usage (hard)

- 81% of Australians take supplements at least occasionally; 38% daily (CMA survey, 2026).
- ABS: 33.6% took a supplement on the survey day (2023), up from 28.5% (2011–12).
- Formats: capsules/tablets 75%, **gummies 24% (up from 20%)**, liquids 21% (up from 13%), powders 21%.

## 7. What looks most promising for a konjac low-cal jelly

1. **Gut / digestive (A$293m, ~9% growth).** Konjac is glucomannan, a prebiotic fibre. A "fibre + gut" jelly fits the second-largest category and avoids the shrinking weight-loss label.
2. **Beauty (A$114m, 14% growth) and mood/sleep (18.6% growth).** These are the fastest-growing categories, and a collagen or magnesium/relax jelly is an easy line extension.
3. **Format tailwind.** Gummies and liquids are the fastest-rising formats, and a jelly sits between them.
4. **Be careful with "weight management" positioning.** It is the only segment shrinking (−7.7% in 2026, −4.3%/yr forecast) because of GLP-1 drugs. Positioning as a fullness / GLP-1 companion snack (fibre, protein, low cal) is safer than a "slimming" claim.
5. **Channel route.** Online and DTC are where growth is (and 40% of sports nutrition), so they suit a launch. Pharmacy is the biggest prize but needs TGA listing if the product makes therapeutic claims. Sold as a food, it would go through supermarkets/convenience under FSANZ rules instead. That food-vs-medicine choice decides the channel.
