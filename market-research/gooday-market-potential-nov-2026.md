# Gooday market potential (21 Nov 2026)

Builds on `au-supplement-sales-by-category-nov-2026.md` and `glp1-weight-loss-trends-nov-2026.md` in this folder. Those files hold the category tables; this one says what they mean for Gooday.

**Product as planned (from the landing pages):** one squeezable fruit jelly pouch a day, either 6 g fibre plus greens at about 25 kcal (superfood) or 5 g collagen with Kakadu plum at about 35 kcal (collagen), pectin or gelatin set (no konjac), sold as a food in Australia. Flavours: green apple, peach, grape.

## Verdict

**Worth pursuing, as a focused online launch, with gut/fibre and "the easy treat" as the lead story.** The demand is real and growing, the format is proven overseas, and nobody in Australia owns a fibre or greens jelly pouch yet. The catch is that a near-identical product just launched at scale in the US, so the window to be first in Australia is probably 12 to 18 months, not open-ended.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is the market big enough? | Yes. A$5.06b domestic supplements in 2026, growing 4.6%. Gummies, liquids and chews are the formats gaining share. |
| Is it growing where Gooday plays? | Yes. Gut A$293m (~9%/yr), beauty/collagen A$114m (14%/yr), and fibre is having a "new protein" moment. Weight management is shrinking, so stay out of that label. |
| Is the format validated? | Yes. Physician's Choice launched a 6 g fibre, 30 kcal squeeze pouch on TikTok Shop on 1 Oct 2026 and was in 4,501 Walmart stores by 24 Oct. |
| Is there a gap in Australia? | Yes, narrowly. Fibre gummies (Swisse, Metamucil) and collagen jellies (Swisse Bella sticks, ATP NOWAY, Muscle Nation) exist, but no daily fibre + greens or fibre + collagen pouch. |
| Biggest risk | Being copied by Swisse or Metamucil, who already sell 6 g fibre gummies in Woolworths, or by Physician's Choice entering Australia. |

## 1. Market size and growth

**Australia (hard data, CMA/Euromonitor):**
- Domestic supplements A$5.06b in 2026 (+4.6%), forecast A$6.96b by 2031 (6.6% CAGR).
- 81% of Australians take a supplement at least occasionally. Nielsen counts 11.8m regular vitamin takers (51% of the population), and category ad spend rose 26.5% to A$75.1m in the year to May 2026, so it is a crowded, well-funded shelf.
- **Format shift is the tailwind.** Gummies rose from 20% to 24% of users and liquids from 13% to 21%, while powders sit at 21%. Gooday is pitched against powders, which is the right side of that trend.
- **Online is 25.8% of vitamin sales and grew 19%/yr since 2021.** Sports nutrition is 40% online. That suits a DTC launch.

**Categories Gooday touches:**

| Pool | Size (AU) | Growth | Fit for Gooday |
|---|---|---|---|
| Digestive / gut | A$293m | ~9%/yr | Strong. 6 g fibre is the core. |
| Beauty (collagen) | A$114m | 14%/yr | Good for the collagen SKU. |
| Greens / superfood powders | No hard figure | Growing; AG1, Bloom, Nutra Organics, GRNS, IM8 all active | Gooday's natural "switch from" target. |
| Sports nutrition | A$1.3b | 7.4% | Secondary; Gooday has no protein or creatine. |
| Weight management | A$0.4b | **−7.7%** | Avoid this label. |

**Fibre is the rising nutrient.** Australians eat about 21 to 25 g fibre a day against targets of 28 to 38 g (ANZ, Sep 2026). 63% of consumers globally say they plan to eat more fibre (Tate & Lyle, 2026), and ANZ's analyst frames it as "fibre is the new protein".

**Global signals (estimates, direction only):**
- Gummy supplements: US$7.7b (IMARC) to US$24.4b (MarketsandMarkets) in 2025, growing 4% to 14%/yr depending on scope. The spread shows how loose these numbers are, but every firm says growth.
- US formats: gummies about 25% of supplement sales; soft chews +34% in 2025; ready-to-drink supplements over US$7b. Industry view: "we're not at peak gummy yet" (NutraIngredients, Jan 2026).
- Jelly sticks are now a manufacturer push (Cosmax, Sirio), which means contract manufacturing is available and more brands will follow.
- GLP-1: mentions in supplement launches up 67% (2023 to 2026). About 500,000 Australians use the drugs regularly, and users shift spend towards protein and fibre and away from sweets.

## 2. Bottom-up: what Gooday could realistically do

Top-down category numbers don't tell you what one new brand can sell. A grounded way to think about it:

- **Price point.** A 30-pouch month at A$55 to A$69 (A$1.80 to A$2.30 a pouch) sits with the benchmarks: The Beauty Chef collagen sachets A$2.90 at full price, ATP NOWAY collagen jelly A$3, US agency-style DTC brands A$40 to A$80 a month.
- **Subscriber maths.** 500 subscribers ≈ A$30k a month; 1,500 ≈ A$90k a month (about A$1.1m a year). That is the range the agency's portfolio brands claim ($10k to $90k a month), so it is a fair "good outcome" for year one, not a forecast.
- **What decides it:** cost per acquired customer versus margin per month and how long subscribers stay. The ad test tracker (target A$10 per signup) is the first real data point. Signups are not purchases, so plan a paid pre-order or first-batch offer to measure real intent.

## 3. Competitors

**Closest product overseas**
- **Physician's Choice Fiber Jelly (US).** Tear-and-squeeze pouch, 6 g prebiotic fibre, 30 kcal, under 1 g sugar, flavours include Peach Mango. TikTok Shop first, then 4,501 Walmart stores in October 2026. This is almost exactly Gooday's spec. It proves demand and gives you a reference to show manufacturers and investors. It also means a funded player could bring it to Australia.

**In Australia today**

| Competitor | What it is | Threat |
|---|---|---|
| Swisse Fibre Boost Gummies (Woolworths) | 6 g fibre per serve (3 gummies), chicory FOS, no added sugar | High. Same fibre claim, biggest brand, already in supermarkets. Gooday's edge is one pouch instead of three gummies, plus greens or collagen. |
| Metamucil Fibre Gummies / Fibre + Probiotic Gummies | Inulin and corn fibre gummies | High in gut. Clinical "regularity" image, older buyer. |
| Swisse Bella Collagen Jelly Sticks | 2,500 mg marine collagen, 15 g stick, contains sugar and HFCS (Asia listing) | Medium. Proves Swisse can do jelly sticks and could bring them here. |
| ATP Science NOWAY Collagen Jelly | 21 g sachet, 16 g collagen protein, A$3, made at home as a dessert | Low to medium. A make-it-yourself jelly, not grab-and-go. |
| Muscle Nation Protein Jelly + Collagen | 25 g protein jelly sachet, A$5, gym channel | Low. Protein dessert for gym-goers. |
| The Jelly Co konjac pouches (Woolworths) | Korean konjac jelly pouch, near zero calories, no stated fibre | Low on function, but shows the pouch format already sells in supermarkets. |
| Greens powders (AG1, Bloom, Nutra Organics, GRNS, IM8) | Powders, A$2 to A$5+ a serve | These are who you take customers from, not who you copy. |

## 4. Gaps Gooday can own

1. **"Fibre you'd actually look forward to."** Fibre products in Australia look and sound medicinal (Metamucil) or are a handful of gummies. A single fruity pouch with a treat feel is open.
2. **Greens without the powder.** Greens powders are popular but the shaker and taste are the complaint the ad concepts already target ("Ditch the powder", "Grey tub"). No Australian greens pouch exists.
3. **Collagen + fibre in one.** Collagen jellies here are either sugary sticks or DIY desserts. A low-sugar daily collagen pouch is a clean line extension into the fastest-growing beauty category.
4. **GLP-1 households as a targeted sub-audience.** Small portion, fibre, low kcal and a sweet hit fit what users say they want. Use it in targeted ads and content, not on the front of pack, because "GLP-1 friendly" badges draw scepticism.

## 5. Risks

| Risk | How bad | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Fast follower (Swisse, Metamucil, Physician's Choice) | High | Move quickly, build a brand and subscriber base online first, and own the taste and treat feel rather than only the fibre number. |
| Claims rules. As a food, claims must follow FSANZ Standard 1.2.7 (only listed food-health relationships, with conditions). Therapeutic claims would make it a TGA product. | High | Get a food regulatory review of label and ad copy before launch. Keep to plain nutrition content ("6 g fibre") and approved general-level claims. No "natural Ozempic" or weight-loss claims. |
| Choking and format safety | Medium | No konjac (already decided, good). Keep the under-5 warning and "squeeze slowly". Ask the manufacturer for texture testing. |
| Unit cost. Pouch filling, cold chain if any, and shipping weight versus capsules | Medium | Get landed cost per pouch from two manufacturers before pricing. Shelf-stable pectin set helps. |
| Customer churn | Medium | Fibre is a habit product, which helps. Use subscribe-and-save, multipacks and flavour rotation. |
| Signups do not equal sales | Medium | Add a paid pre-order or deposit step after the ad test. |
| Crowded, well-funded ad market (ad spend +26.5%) | Medium | Stick to the narrow, specific angles the agency playbook recommends rather than broad "health" messaging. |

## 6. Recommendation and next steps

1. **Lead with gut/fibre plus "easy treat"**, with greens as the supporting reason. Keep collagen as the second SKU, decided by the ad test.
2. **Launch online (DTC, then Amazon AU / TikTok Shop)** before approaching Woolworths or Coles. Supermarkets come once you have sell-through data.
3. **Finish the ad test, then run a paid pre-order** for the winning angle. A reasonable go signal is a cost per signup at or under A$10 and at least 100 to 200 paid pre-orders.
4. **Book a food regulatory review** of the label and ad claims.
5. **Get two manufacturer quotes** for a pectin-set, shelf-stable pouch and work out margin at A$1.80 to A$2.30 a pouch.
6. **Watch Physician's Choice** for an Australian launch and Swisse for a fibre or collagen jelly move.
