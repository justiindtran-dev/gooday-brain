# Gooday: who buys it and which creators can reach them (21 Nov 2026)

Builds on the avatars thread (11 Nov), `gooday-market-potential-nov-2026.md`, `glp1-weight-loss-trends-nov-2026.md` and the packaging critique. The avatars were written for the old konjac protein jelly. The product is now a pectin-set pouch with 6 g fibre + greens (~25 kcal, green apple and grape) and a 5 g collagen peach line (~35 kcal), so the segments below are re-cut for that.

## Short answer

- **Lead buyer:** women 25 to 45 who already pay for a greens or collagen powder and are tired of the shaker, plus the "fibre goals" crowd who want an easy daily fibre habit. This is who the ad test's B angles (Ditch the powder, Grey tub, Your gut called) already speak to.
- **Second:** macro-counting gym-goers who want a 25 kcal sweet hit. Good for the "easy treat" angle and the A-1 / A-3 ads.
- **Third:** busy parents (mostly mums) who want a grab-and-go treat for themselves.
- **Handle carefully:** GLP-1 households. Real fit, but no creator content and no drug mentions.
- **Best creator mix to start:** 2 to 3 gut-health or sports dietitians for credibility, 4 to 6 micro fitness and wellness creators for volume, 1 to 2 food reviewers for the taste test. Gift first, unscripted, as the agency playbook suggests, then pay the ones whose posts convert.

## 3. Who to avoid

- **Weight-loss transformation accounts**, even big Australian ones in the diet rankings (for example @brittanymccrystal, @ghallfitness, @alexgamblecoach, @heymama.lanz). Their audience fits segment 6, but their content frames everything as weight loss, which pulls Gooday into a shrinking category and into claims you can't make.
- **Creators with their own supplement or vitamin brand** (for example @jshealth, @tommyshealthaddictsvegan). Conflict of interest and unlikely to post.
- **Anyone with a history of anti-medical or misinformation content.** Check before gifting.
- **Kids' content.** The pouch format looks like a kids' food pouch, and the pack carries a not-for-under-5s warning. Keep children out of creator content.

## 4. Audience and compliance risks

1. **Weight-loss and GLP-1 framing.** Weight management is shrinking (−7.7%) and draws scepticism. Prescription medicines cannot be advertised to the public in Australia, so creators must not name Ozempic, Mounjaro or Wegovy alongside Gooday, even casually ("my Ozempic snack"). No "natural Ozempic", "appetite suppressant" or "lose weight" language. Reach GLP-1 households only through neutral targeted ads ("small, sweet, 6 g fibre") and leave creators out of it.
2. **Health claims (FSANZ Standard 1.2.7).** Creators can say what's in it ("6 g fibre", "plus greens", "5 g collagen", "25 kcal") and how it tastes and fits their day. They can't say it fixes bloating, heals the gut, boosts immunity or energy, or improves skin, hair or joints. Anything therapeutic could make it a TGA product. A creator's words count as your advertising, so give every creator a one-page "can say / can't say" brief and approve posts before they go live.
3. **Influencer disclosure.** Gifted and paid posts must be clearly labelled (#ad or the platform's paid-partnership tag) under the AANA Code and ACCC rules. The ACCC has been sweeping influencer posts for hidden ads. Gifting counts.
4. **Health professionals.** Dietitians endorsing a product are bound by Dietitians Australia's code (no misleading claims, disclose payment). If you ever work with a doctor, nurse or pharmacist, AHPRA rules bar testimonials about health outcomes, so keep their posts to "what's in it" only.
5. **Gut-health creators will check the fibre.** If the fibre is inulin or chicory, people with IBS may react (high FODMAP). Know the fibre source and FODMAP status before contacting Nicole Dynan or similar, or they'll decline or post a caveat.
6. **Collagen.** Declare fish (marine collagen) clearly; vegan and plant-based creators like Tess Begg suit the fibre line only.
7. **Follower quality.** Some ranking pages show identical audience stats across creators, so their numbers may be templated. Before paying anyone, ask for a screenshot of their audience insights (aim for 60%+ Australian, 25 to 44, mostly women for segments 1, 2, 4 and 5).

## 5. Suggested first step

Gift a 14-pouch bag to about 15 creators (3 dietitians, 8 fitness and wellness micros, 2 reviewers, 2 parents), unscripted, with the claims brief and a unique discount code each. Track signups by code in the ad test tracker. Pay or re-engage the top 3 by signups. Budget is mostly product and postage at this stage.
