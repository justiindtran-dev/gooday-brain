## Realism: which cues helped most (reuse these)
Ranked by how much each one moved "looks real":
1. **A real photographed pouch as the base (biggest jump, 3 to 4).** It brings the real outline, the seal edges with crimp, the V-notch fold, and the real cast and contact shadow on a real surface. No render cue came close.
2. **Real highlights kept and screen-blended over the new art.** A white top-hat of the photo, keeping only long thin streaks (more than 40 px, elongated) and dropping dots so no printed droplets survive. Real crinkle highlights on the seals sell "film".
3. **Real low-frequency shading multiplied into the art** at 1.4 to 1.6x contrast, with the artwork areas filled by wide normalised convolution. Use that, not Telea inpainting, which leaves blotches.
4. **A white-print shoulder.** Printed white is capped at the photo's own white-print level (knee 150, slope 0.55). A blown-out white panel was the most "AI" thing in v1.
5. **Fruit over the soft-focused claim panel.** A blurred text block on an otherwise sharp, flat-lying pouch is an instant tell, so let real fruit cover it. Hide the cut-out's frame-cut side off the canvas edge.
6. **Fruit light matching:** a warm multiply plus side fall-off toward the light side, and a cast shadow in the same direction as the pouch's real shadow.

Helped little at ad size:
- Halftone dots and 1 px ink misregistration (invisible after downscaling, but harmless).
- Spot-gloss logo (helps only in close-ups).

Hurt when overdone (tested on the render, `source/real/packtest_render_old_vs_film.png`):
- Fine crinkle noise reads as hammered foil.
- Curved scuffs read as hairs or lint. Keep scuffs straight, short and faint.
- Strong environment reflection greys out the pack.
