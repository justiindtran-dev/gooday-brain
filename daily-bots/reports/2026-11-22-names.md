# Brand name shortlist (Marketing boss) - 2026-11-22

Product: daily squeeze jelly pouches (collagen + Kakadu plum vitamin C, peach; fibre + greens, green apple). Audience: busy Australians 25-45, mostly women, tired of powders, pills and shakers. Positioning: one easy daily habit, tasty, no mixing.

**Read this first:** every availability note below is a quick, unverified desk check (web search only), not legal advice. Live DNS/WHOIS lookups were blocked from this environment, so domain notes are judgement calls ("appears taken" / "appears free / unverified"). Instagram/TikTok could not be loaded directly; handle notes are from search results only. Trademark screening (IP Australia, classes 5, 29, 30, 32) is happening separately and must be done before anything is bought. Nothing was registered, signed up for or contacted.

Earlier work read: Notion "Brand names and checks" (Gooday kept as working name; gooday.com taken since 1999; Squeezday and Onepouch as backups; J'ello dropped; G'DAY marks in food classes flagged as the main Gooday risk).

Names screened out on the way: **Wobbl** (too close to Wibble, UK jelly pots), **Habbit** (Habbit Health, Indian nutrition brand, and "Habbit jelly" results), **Eazyday** (too close to Everydaze, US collagen jelly sticks, and easydays.com.au), **Sweet As** (says "sugar", wrong for a health product), **Glowday** (reads as a skin claim).

## Top 3 (ranked)

**1. Squeezday.** The best all-rounder. It is the only name that says both the format (squeeze, no mixing) and the habit (every day), so it does positioning work on pack and in ads with no explanation, and it can never be read as a medical or therapeutic claim. It is a coined word, which usually helps distinctiveness at IP Australia, and quick checks found no food or supplement brand using it, with both domains appearing free (unverified). It travels to the US as "squeeze the day". The watch-out: US marks for SQUEEZE THE DAY and SQUEEZD exist, so the US check matters before that market.

**2. Righto.** The strongest brand voice for Australia: warm, cheeky, and it says "done" in a way that fits a habit busy people tick off. Great for copy ("Righto, that's today sorted"). The risks are that it is a common word, so the .com and handles are probably taken and the trade mark may need a device or a longer form, and IP Australia pages came up in search (contents not checked).

**3. Goodo.** Closest in spirit to Gooday (Aussie, positive, short) but sidesteps the G'DAY food-class risk flagged in Notion. It is easy to say and spell in the US. The main risk is Good-O Beverages in the US drinks category, plus Goodio as a sound-alike, so it is stronger as an AU-only name than a global one.

**Against Gooday:** Gooday still has the warmest local hook. If the separate trade mark check clears it in classes 5/29/30/32, keep it. If it does not, Squeezday is the recommended switch (the same fallback the Notion page named).
