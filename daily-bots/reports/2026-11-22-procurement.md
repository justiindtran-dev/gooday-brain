# Procurement bot: jelly pouch contract manufacturers

**Sunday 22 Nov 2026.** This report uses public web information only. Nobody was contacted, no forms were submitted and no quotes were requested.

**Spec being sourced:** a single-serve squeezable jelly in a pouch or stick. There are two SKUs:

- Fibre and greens: 6 g fibre, about 25 kcal.
- Collagen and Kakadu plum: 5 g collagen, about 35 kcal.

The gel must be pectin, gelatin or gellan. **No konjac.** It is sold as a food in Australia.

**How to read the figures.** "Published" means the figure is on the supplier's own page or listing, with the source linked. **EST** means it is my estimate and has not been published or confirmed.

## Bottom line

1. **No Australian maker publicly offers a ready-made functional jelly pouch.** Australian options are co-packers. They would fill a jelly that Gooday or a formulator develops into sachets, sticks or spouted pouches. Expect a higher unit cost, but you get a "Made in Australia" story and a lower MOQ.
2. **The strongest published capability is in Taiwan:** TCI and Wel-Bloom. China comes next: ATIQ and Litafood. Korea has jelly-stick experts, but most published MOQs are high.
3. **Konjac is everywhere in Asian spout-pouch jelly.** Most Chinese and Vietnamese "jelly drink pouch" listings are konjac. Several suppliers that pass on konjac use carrageenan instead (HNK, KG Lab). Carrageenan is not on Gooday's approved list. Any RFQ must say "pectin, gelatin or gellan only, no konjac, no carrageenan unless approved".
4. **Format matters more than country.** A 15 to 25 g tear-and-squeeze stick is what Physician's Choice uses and what Taiwanese and Korean factories make. It costs less than a capped spouted pouch (EST). A 100 to 150 g capped spouted pouch is the Chinese and Thai "jelly drink" format, and it is too big for a 25 kcal serve.

## Things to check whatever the supplier

- **Imported gelatin or collagen:** bovine and porcine material needs a DAFF BICON import check or permit. Marine collagen is usually simpler (EST, verify on BICON). Halal certification matters if gelatin is used.
- **Food labelling:** fibre claims need FSANZ Standard 1.2.7 wording. Imported product falls under the Imported Food Inspection Scheme.
- **Choking safety:** ask for texture or viscosity data and a squeeze-test. Keep the under-5 warning.
- **Certificates:** ask for current copies of ISO 22000, HACCP, halal and BRC. Several listings above contradict themselves.

## Sand-M (Thailand): field find, Bangkok 22 Nov

Justin photographed this product in Bangkok today. Research uses public web info and the pack photos only. **Nobody was contacted.**

### The product: HandyHerb "Jelly Me Cuti Activ Fiber"

- **What it is:** a 30 g tear-and-squeeze **fibre jelly stick**, green apple and blackcurrant flavour. In Thailand it is registered as a *dietary supplement* (ผลิตภัณฑ์เสริมอาหาร), not a confectionery. The pack says to take one before bed.
- **Nutrition per stick (from the pack):** 7 g fibre (28% Thai RDI), 0 g sugar, about 16 g total carbohydrate, sodium 65 mg, potassium 120 mg. It is low-kcal; the exact kcal figure is not legible in the photo.
- **Fibre source:** about 6.8 g of a "90% fibre" ingredient. Watsons lists it as **gum arabic (acacia) fibre**. There are also small apple and blackcurrant extracts.
- **Sweeteners:** sucralose and acesulfame K.
- **Gel system:** **konjac, carrageenan and locust bean gum.** This gel cannot be sold in Australia. The ACCC ban covers konjac mini-cup jellies; check before relying on it for a stick, but Gooday's rule is no konjac anyway.
- **Pack codes:** Thai FDA number about **12-1-02261-5-0207**, read from the photo, so verify it. The "12-1-02261" prefix means a Nonthaburi establishment making the product itself (EST interpretation). Barcode 8856082005633.
- **Positioning:** a cute mascot (a winking green-apple character), "0% Sugar", a gut icon and "easy to eat, tasty" claims. It is aimed at young Thai women and impulse buys at pharmacies and convenience stores. "Cuti" sits in the Jelly Me range with collagen and vitamin jelly variants.
- **Price in Thailand:** **฿20 per 30 g stick at Watsons TH** (published, [Watsons](https://www.watsons.co.th/en/handy-herb-handy-herb-jelly-me-cuti-fiber-30g./p/BP_318844)). That is about A$0.85 at ฿23.5 per A$1 (EST FX).

**What Gooday can learn from it**
1. **The format is proven.** A 30 g stick can carry 7 g of fibre at about ฿20 retail. That suggests ex-factory cost is well under ฿8–10, roughly A$0.35–0.45 (EST). This supports the EST cost range in the table above.
2. **Acacia (gum arabic) fibre** carries a 7 g dose in a small gel without the grittiness of inulin. It is a good fibre to put in the RFQ alongside inulin and FOS.
3. **Konjac plus carrageenan is how they get a firm, sliceable set at high fibre load.** Switching to pectin or gellan with 6–7 g of soluble fibre needs a real R&D trial. Ask for a texture and syneresis test, not just a quote.
4. **Branding:** cute, bright and "0% sugar" works in Asia. For Australia, Gooday's "wellness in your pocket" tone should be more adult. Note that Sand-M runs a **range** (fibre, collagen, vitamins) under one jelly sub-brand, which is the same play as Gooday's two SKUs.
