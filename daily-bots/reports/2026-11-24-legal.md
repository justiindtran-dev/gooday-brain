## 1. Supplier NDA: when (EST guidance)
- **The RFQ itself is fine without an NDA.** It shared a general spec (ingredient types, round doses, formats, flavours), not the recipe. That's normal for a first enquiry.
- **Sign an NDA before sending:** the exact formula from formulation.md (grams and % w/w per ingredient), the gel system details, artwork files (pack PDFs or AI files), or any of our test data. Typical trigger: a supplier says "send us your formula for the sample". **Pause and ask Justin then.**
- **Which NDA:** ours (`daily-bots/legal/supplier-mutual-nda-draft.md`) or theirs. Many factories send their own; compare them on mutual obligations, term (at least 3 to 5 years), what counts as confidential, **no reverse-engineering or selling our formula to others**, and governing law.
- **China suppliers (ATIQ, Litafood, Victory, JoyGlo, Guangzhou Green Health):** a plain Western NDA is hard to enforce there. Lawyers usually suggest an **NNN agreement** (non-disclosure, non-use, non-circumvention) in Chinese and English, under Chinese law with a Chinese court or arbitration. **Lawyer needed** before signing with a Chinese factory (EST A$500 to 1,500).
- **Before any NDA is signed:** Gooday should exist as the contracting party (structure is an open decision). If Justin signs before the company exists, sign "for and on behalf of a company to be formed" and assign later (lawyer to confirm), and make sure the co-founder agreement covers the IP.

## 2. Samples into Australia: collagen and gelatin (check before anyone pays for samples)
- **Commercial samples follow commercial rules.** Agriculture says "Commercial samples are treated in the same manner as commercial quantities" and must meet the commercial conditions in BICON ([Agriculture biological imports FAQ](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/goods/biological/faq)). There is no "it's only a sample" exemption.
- **Collagen and gelatin are animal products**, so **check BICON for the exact product** (shelf-stable food with bovine or fish collagen, or gelatin) before samples ship. The result may be: no permit if the right documents travel with the goods, **or** an import permit that must be issued before shipping (Agriculture says most permits take about **20 working days** after a complete, paid application; [FAQ](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/goods/biological/faq)). Permit cost EST A$120 to 400.
- **Documents must be recent.** Manufacturer's declarations and product questionnaires must meet Agriculture's documentary rules, including being **signed and dated within the last 6 months** ([FAQ](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/goods/biological/faq); [minimum document requirements](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/sitecollectiondocuments/biosecurity/import/general-info/documentary-requirements/minimum-document-requirements-policy.pdf)).
- **Bovine:** beef products are "risk food" for BSE, but **gelatine and collagen from hides and skins are exempt** ([Agriculture](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/import/goods/food/inspection-compliance/risk-food/beef)). Ask each supplier in writing: species, tissue (hide/skin vs bone) and country of origin of the collagen.
- **Marine (fish):** separate BICON conditions, and a fish allergen declaration.
- **Fibre-only samples** (plant ingredients, shelf-stable) are likely simpler (EST). **Safest order:** fibre samples first; collagen samples only from the 2 or 3 finalists, once the BICON check is done and their documents are ready.
- **With every sample parcel, the supplier should send:** commercial invoice marked "samples, no commercial value" **with a realistic value** (never under-declare), a full ingredient list with %, the collagen/gelatin source declaration (species, tissue, origin, signed and dated), and a CoA. Keep each consignment under A$1,000 (EST: avoids a full import declaration).
- **Who does it:** Justin runs the BICON check (free, online) or asks a customs broker (EST A$150 to 400). The bot never applies or pays.

## 3. What to ask suppliers for (sample stage; draft list)
Put this in the reply to any supplier that answers with a quote. Compliance: PASS (no claims).
1. **Certificates** (HACCP, ISO 22000 / FSSC 22000 / BRC, GMP, halal): copy with **certificate number, issuing body and expiry**. We verify each with the issuer.
2. **Product spec sheet** for the sample (ingredients with %, gel system, pH, °Brix, water activity, shelf life).
3. **CoA** for the sample batch: micro (TPC, yeast and mould, E. coli, Salmonella), heavy metals (lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury), plus fibre or protein content and the method used.
4. **Allergen statement** for the product **and the line**, against the Australian list: peanut, tree nuts, egg, milk, sesame, soy, fish, crustacea, mollusc, wheat, gluten (barley, oats, rye), lupin, and sulphites ≥10 mg/kg ([FSANZ PEAL](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/food-allergies/allergen-labelling)).
5. **Written "no konjac, no glucomannan, no carrageenan"** statement, including cross-contact on the line.
6. **Collagen/gelatin source declaration**: species, tissue, country, BSE/TSE statement, halal status.
7. Country of origin of each main ingredient, and whether they can issue an **FTA certificate of origin** for Australia.
8. Factory street address and the latest **third-party audit report** (especially ATIQ and Litafood, which share an office address).
9. Product liability insurance certificate (needed later for the supply agreement).
