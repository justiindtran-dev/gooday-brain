## 1. Quick answers (to confirm with a consultant)

**Can we add vitamins or minerals? Probably not.**
- Std 1.3.2 says vitamins or minerals may only be added to the foods listed in Schedule 17 (S17-4 table).
- Jelly, confectionery and water-based desserts are **not listed**. The only dessert entry is dairy desserts (3.1% or more milk protein).
- Vitamin C can be added only to juices, fruit drinks, cordials, formulated beverages and cereals.
- So Kakadu plum has to deliver vitamin C as a **whole-food ingredient**, not as added ascorbic acid "as a vitamin".
- Schedule 17: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00449/2026-04-30/2026-04-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html
- Std 1.3.2: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00400/2021-03-26/2021-03-26/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html

**General food, sports food or TGA medicine?**
- **General food** is the likely default: a pouch eaten as a snack, with no therapeutic claims.
- **Formulated supplementary sports food** (Std 2.9.4) only applies if the product is "specifically formulated to assist sports people in achieving specific nutritional or performance goals". If it is one:
  - prescribed name "Formulated supplementary sports food"
  - warnings: not suitable for under 15s or pregnant women; use under medical or dietetic supervision; not a sole source of nutrition; use with a training program
  - vitamins and minerals then allowed under S29-16
  - Wrong fit for a wellness snack. FSANZ review P1010 is still open; the page was last updated Feb 2025.
  - Std 2.9.4: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00421/2026-06-09/2026-06-09/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html
  - P1010: https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/food-standards-code/proposals/P1010
- **TGA listed medicine** is the risk if we:
  - present the pouch as a dose
  - use supplement language ("daily dose", "take one")
  - make therapeutic claims ("supports joints", "glowing skin from within")
  - TGA's food-medicine tool decides this. Some sports supplements in medicinal dosage forms became medicines from 30 Nov 2023.
  - TGA tool (FMIGT): https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/decision-trees/food-medicine-interface-guidance-tool-fmigt
  - Sports supplements declared medicines: https://www.tga.gov.au/community-qa/sports-supplements-declared-be-medicines
  - Note: the TGA site blocked automated reads today. These links came from search results and I did not read the pages.

**Fibre claims (Std 1.2.7 / Schedule 4)**
- "Source of fibre": at least **2 g** per serve. "Good source": at least **4 g**. "Excellent source": at least **7 g**.
- Pre-approved health claim: "**Contributes to regular laxation**". The food must meet the fibre content claim conditions.
- Every health claim also needs the product to pass the **Nutrient Profiling Scoring Criterion (NPSC)**. Sugar levels could fail it, so ask.
- Schedule 4: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00474/2024-10-29/2024-10-29/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html
- Std 1.2.7: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00394/2024-10-29/2024-10-29/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html
- FSANZ claims guide: https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/labelling/nutrition-health-and-related-claims

**Collagen claims**
- **No collagen health claim exists** in Schedule 4.
- Collagen can only support **protein** claims: "source of protein" needs at least **5 g** per serve, "good source" at least **10 g**. The pre-approved protein claims cover tissue building and repair, muscle and bones.
- "Contains collagen" may be fine as a plain ingredient statement. Ask about this, and about characterising ingredient %.
- The better route is **vitamin C** from Kakadu plum:
  - "Contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin" is a pre-approved vitamin C claim
  - it needs at least **10% RDI** per serve (about 4 mg); "good source" needs at least 25%
  - Ask whether a naturally occurring amount qualifies.

**Labelling basics** (Ch. 1.2 of the Food Standards Code). Ask the consultant to check:
- name or description
- ingredient list with characterising ingredient % (Kakadu plum, greens, collagen)
- NIP per serve and per 100 g
- allergens in PEAL format (gelatin source; any fish or marine collagen)
- best-before date and storage
- supplier details
- Country of Origin label (ACCC information standard)
- optional Health Star Rating
- FSANZ code overview: https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/labelling/nutrition-health-and-related-claims

**Choking and gel texture**
- ACCC permanent ban (Consumer Protection Notice No. 2 of 2004) covers **mini jelly cups containing konjac, 45 mm or less in height or width**.
- Konjac is also listed as glucomannan, konnyaku, taro powder and yam flour.
- Jellies without konjac are "not considered dangerous". The page says nothing about pouches or squeeze formats.
- We use no konjac (pectin, gelatin and gellan), so the ban should not apply. Even so, ask:
  - gel firmness and texture for a suck-from-pouch format
  - choking warnings
  - spout size
  - children
  - general ACL safety duty
  - whether Std 1.3.1 / Schedule 15 restricts konjac in jelly confectionery (I could not confirm this today)
- ACCC ban page: https://www.productsafety.gov.au/business/find-banned-products/mini-jelly-cups-containing-konjac-ban
- Import prohibition: https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/goods/food/type/mini-jelly-cups
